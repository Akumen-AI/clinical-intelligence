# Insurance Rules: Pre-Authorization and Claims Submission

## Section 1: Purpose

This document establishes internal rules for verifying insurance coverage, obtaining pre-authorization, and submitting claims for hospital services, to reduce claim denials and ensure timely reimbursement.

## Section 2: Insurance Verification

2.1. Insurance eligibility must be verified within 24 hours of a scheduled admission, or before discharge for unplanned/emergency admissions.

2.2. Verification must confirm: active coverage status, applicable deductible and co-pay amounts, and whether the admitting facility is in-network for the patient's plan.

2.3. Self-pay patients (no active insurance) must be referred to the financial counseling desk before elective procedures.

## Section 3: Pre-Authorization Requirements

3.1. The following service categories require pre-authorization from the payer before the service is rendered, except in emergencies: elective surgical procedures, non-emergency MRI/CT imaging, inpatient admissions exceeding 3 days, and durable medical equipment over a defined cost threshold.

3.2. Pre-authorization requests must be submitted at least 5 business days before a scheduled elective procedure.

3.3. Emergency admissions are exempt from pre-authorization at time of service, but retrospective notification to the payer is required within 24–48 hours depending on payer-specific rules.

3.4. A denied pre-authorization must be routed to the utilization review team for a peer-to-peer appeal before the service is cancelled or rescheduled, where clinically appropriate.

## Section 4: Claims Submission

4.1. Claims must be submitted within the payer-specific filing deadline; where no specific deadline is on file, the default internal target is submission within 7 days of discharge.

4.2. All claims must include correctly mapped ICD-10 diagnosis codes and CPT/HCPCS procedure codes matching the documentation in the patient's discharge record.

4.3. Claims for services requiring pre-authorization must include the authorization reference number; claims missing this reference will be held for correction before submission, not submitted incomplete.

## Section 5: Denials and Appeals

5.1. Claim denials must be reviewed by the billing team within 3 business days of receipt.

5.2. Denials categorized as "medical necessity" require clinical documentation review and may involve the treating physician in the appeal narrative.

5.3. Denials categorized as "coding error" or "missing information" should be corrected and resubmitted rather than formally appealed, where the payer allows resubmission.

5.4. All appeals must be filed within the payer's appeal window; internal target is filing within 10 business days of the denial notice to preserve buffer time before the payer deadline.

## Section 6: Patient Financial Responsibility

6.1. Patients must be informed of estimated out-of-pocket costs for elective procedures before the procedure is scheduled, where feasible.

6.2. Financial counseling must be offered to any patient whose estimated out-of-pocket responsibility exceeds a threshold defined annually by the Revenue Cycle department.
