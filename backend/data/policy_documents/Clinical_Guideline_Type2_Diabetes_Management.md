## Section 1: Purpose and Applicability

This guideline provides a standardized approach to glycemic management for adult patients with a documented history of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) admitted for any inpatient reason, including those not primarily admitted for diabetes-related complications.

## Section 2: Initial Assessment

2.1. All patients with a documented T2DM history must have an HbA1c result on file within the last 90 days; if unavailable, order on admission.

2.2. Home medication list must be reconciled within 4 hours of admission, per Clinical Guideline CG-004 (Medication Reconciliation).

2.3. Assess for hypoglycemia risk factors: renal impairment, poor oral intake, concurrent insulin and sulfonylurea use.

## Section 3: Glycemic Targets

3.1. Target blood glucose range for most non-critically ill inpatients: 140–180 mg/dL.

3.2. For patients in critical care settings, target range: 140–180 mg/dL, with tighter control (110–140 mg/dL) considered only on attending physician order.

3.3. Avoid targets below 110 mg/dL for general ward patients due to elevated hypoglycemia risk without proportional benefit.

## Section 4: Monitoring Frequency

4.1. Point-of-care glucose checks: before each meal and at bedtime for patients on subcutaneous insulin.

4.2. Every 4–6 hours for patients on continuous IV insulin infusion, or per unit-specific critical care protocol.

4.3. Any glucose reading below 70 mg/dL requires immediate hypoglycemia protocol activation (see Section 6) and a repeat check within 15 minutes.

## Section 5: Medication Considerations

5.1. Metformin should generally be held on admission if renal function is impaired (eGFR < 30) or if the patient is scheduled for a contrast-based imaging procedure within 48 hours.

5.2. Sulfonylureas carry elevated hypoglycemia risk in patients with reduced oral intake; consider holding if the patient is NPO (nothing by mouth) status.

5.3. Basal-bolus insulin regimens are generally preferred over sliding-scale-only regimens for patients with an HbA1c above 8%.

## Section 6: Hypoglycemia Protocol

6.1. For glucose 54–69 mg/dL with the patient conscious and able to swallow: administer 15g fast-acting oral carbohydrate, recheck in 15 minutes.

6.2. For glucose below 54 mg/dL or patient unable to swallow safely: administer IV dextrose or IM glucagon per unit protocol, notify physician immediately.

6.3. Document every hypoglycemic event, its treatment, and the recheck value in the patient's chart.

## Section 7: Discharge Considerations

7.1. Reassess and adjust home diabetes regimen based on inpatient glucose trends before discharge.

7.2. Provide diabetes education materials and confirm patient/caregiver understanding of any medication changes.

7.3. Arrange endocrinology or primary care follow-up within 2 weeks for patients with significant regimen changes during admission.
