# **1\. Purpose and Scope**

This document establishes the official policies and standard operating procedures (SOPs) governing all clinical, administrative, and operational activities at Metropolitan General Hospital (MGH). It is designed to serve as the primary structured knowledge base for the hospital's AI-powered Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) chatbot system, enabling consistent, accurate, and policy-compliant responses to queries from clinical staff, administrative personnel, and management.

## **1.1 Organizational Mission**

Metropolitan General Hospital is committed to delivering safe, high-quality, patient-centred healthcare to the communities it serves. This document operationalises that commitment by codifying the standards and expectations that guide every staff member's professional conduct.

## **1.2 Scope of Application**

This policy applies universally to all individuals operating within hospital premises or under hospital authority, including:

**•** Full-time, part-time, and casual clinical staff (physicians, nurses, allied health professionals, technicians)

**•** Administrative, clerical, and billing staff

**•** Support services (environmental services, food and nutrition, transport, maintenance)

**•** Contracted vendors, agency staff, and locum personnel

**•** Students, interns, residents, and fellows on placement

**•** Volunteers and pastoral care providers

# **2\. Patient Admission and Registration**

## **2.1 Emergency Department Admissions**

All patients presenting to the Emergency Department (ED) receive immediate triage assessment using the Emergency Severity Index (ESI) five-level system. The triage nurse must assign an ESI level within five minutes of patient arrival. Resuscitation bays are reserved for ESI Level 1 patients.

**1\.** Patient arrival logged in ED tracking system; wristband printed with name, DOB, and Medical Record Number (MRN).

**2\.** Triage nurse records chief complaint, vital signs, pain score (0–10), and ESI level in EHR.

**3\.** ESI 1–2: Attending physician notified immediately; patient moved to treatment bay.

**4\.** ESI 3–5: Patient directed to appropriate care zone; reassessment documented every 60 minutes until discharge or admission.

**5\.** Registration staff complete demographic and insurance data capture within 30 minutes.

**6\.** HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices provided; acknowledgment signed or documented as refused.

**7\.** If inpatient admission required, admitting physician contacted per on-call schedule within 30 minutes of decision.

## **2.2 Scheduled / Elective Admissions**

**•** Pre-Admission Testing (PAT) completed 1–7 days before procedure: labs, ECG, imaging, anaesthesia review.

**•** Insurance pre-authorisation confirmed by Patient Access team before day of admission.

**•** Patient reports to Admissions Desk; photo ID and insurance card verified; co-pay collected if applicable.

**•** Consent for treatment signed and witnessed; copy provided to patient and scanned into EHR within 2 hours.

**•** Nursing admission assessment completed within 2 hours of bed assignment.

**•** Advance directives reviewed; documented under patient safety flags in EHR.

**•** Primary care physician notified of admission within 4 hours.

## **2.3 Paediatric Admissions**

Paediatric patients (under 18) require consent from a parent or legally authorised guardian except in life-threatening emergencies. A dedicated paediatric nurse assesses all patients under 12 upon admission. Child life specialists are available 7 days a week to support paediatric patients and families.

## **2.5 Discharge Planning**

Discharge planning begins at admission for all inpatients. The interdisciplinary care team — including the attending physician, primary nurse, case manager, and social worker — collaborates to identify discharge barriers early. Discharge criteria must be documented in the EHR before patient departure. The following are required for discharge:

**•** Written discharge summary provided to patient and forwarded to primary care physician within 24 hours.

**•** Medication reconciliation completed; discharge prescription(s) sent electronically to pharmacy of patient's choice.

**•** Follow-up appointments scheduled before leaving; patient education on diagnosis and medications documented.

**•** Transport arranged for patients who cannot self-transport; social work involved for complex social needs.

# **3\. Clinical Care Standards**

## **3.1 Medication Administration — Five Rights**

All licensed clinical staff administering medications must verify the following Five Rights prior to every administration event. Failure to complete this verification constitutes a medication safety event and must be reported via SERS.

**•** Right Patient — verify using two identifiers: full name AND date of birth or MRN

**•** Right Medication — confirm drug name, concentration, and dosage form against the Medication Administration Record (MAR)

**•** Right Dose — cross-check calculated dose with the physician's order and pharmacy label

**•** Right Route — confirm that the route matches the order and is appropriate for the patient

**•** Right Time — administer within ±30 minutes of scheduled time for time-critical medications

_High-alert medications — including anticoagulants, insulins, opioids, concentrated electrolytes (KCl, NaCl >0.9%), and chemotherapy agents — require an independent double-check by a second licensed nurse before administration. Both nurses must sign the MAR._

## **3.2 Fall Prevention Protocol**

**1\.** Morse Fall Scale completed on admission, post-procedure, following a change in condition, and every 8 hours.

**2\.** Score ≥ 45: High fall risk — apply yellow fall risk wristband and post FALL RISK signage on room door.

**3\.** Bed in lowest position, wheels locked; call light and personal items within reach at all times.

**4\.** Non-slip footwear provided; patient and family educated on fall prevention at admission and daily.

**5\.** Hourly intentional rounding documented in EHR by assigned nurse.

**6\.** Assistive devices (walkers, bed rails) assessed and available for high-risk patients.

**7\.** Any fall — with or without injury — reported in SERS within 1 hour and assessed by physician within 2 hours.

## **3.3 Pressure Injury Prevention**

Braden Scale scored at admission, every 72 hours for stable patients, and within 24 hours of any significant change in condition. Patients scoring ≤ 18 are classified at risk.

**•** Reposition minimum every 2 hours; document turn in EHR.

**•** Pressure-relieving mattress ordered via Central Supply for scores ≤ 16.

**•** Head-of-bed elevated no more than 30° unless clinically required (e.g., post-cardiac cath, GERD).

**•** Skin inspection head-to-toe at each shift; Stage 2+ wounds photographed and documented.

**•** Wound Care Nurse consulted for Stage 2 or higher; Nutritionist consulted for scores ≤ 14.

## **3.4 Pain Management**

Pain assessment performed using validated tools: Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for alert adults, FLACC for paediatrics, and CPOT for non-verbal or intubated patients. Pain reassessment required within 60 minutes of any pharmacological intervention and within 30 minutes of a procedure.

**•** Non-pharmacological interventions (repositioning, heat/cold, relaxation) documented before escalating analgesia.

**•** Opioid administration requires pain score documentation pre- and post-administration.

**•** Patients on PCA (Patient-Controlled Analgesia) assessed hourly for sedation, respiratory rate, and SpO₂.

**•** Acute pain service (APS) consulted for all complex pain cases, including post-surgical pain requiring >48 hours of opioid therapy.

# **4\. Nursing Protocols and Documentation**

## **4.1 Shift Handover — SBAR Format**

All clinical handovers must use the Situation-Background-Assessment-Recommendation (SBAR) communication framework. Bedside handover is mandatory for all inpatient ward patients. EHR must be reviewed together during handover; outstanding tasks must be verbally communicated and not solely relying on written documentation.

**•** Situation: Patient name, MRN, diagnosis, current condition

**•** Background: Relevant history, medications, allergies, procedures performed

**•** Assessment: Current vital signs, pain score, clinical concerns, IV access

**•** Recommendation: Pending orders, follow-up needed, escalation concerns

## **4.2 IV Access and Infusion Safety**

**1\.** Peripheral IV cannulas assessed for patency, site integrity, and infection at each shift.

**2\.** Cannulas in situ > 72 hours rotated to a new site unless clinically not possible (document rationale).

**3\.** All IV infusions programmed via Smart Pump with DERS (Drug Error Reduction Software) active.

**4\.** Free-flow IV infusion without pump is prohibited for all medications.

**5\.** Central line dressing changes performed aseptically every 7 days or when soiled/dislodged.

**6\.** CLABSI bundle compliance (maximal barrier precautions, chlorhexidine prep, optimal site selection) documented for each central line insertion.

## **4.3 EHR Documentation Standards**

All patient care activities must be documented in the Electronic Health Record (Epic) in real time or within 30 minutes of the event. Late entries must be flagged as such with reason. Backdating documentation is prohibited and constitutes a compliance violation.

**•** Assessment: Full nursing assessment within 2 hours of shift commencement.

**•** Interventions: Documented immediately following completion.

**•** Medications: MAR signed immediately post-administration; never signed in advance.

**•** Patient education: Content, method, and patient understanding documented at each encounter.

**•** Communication: All verbal orders read back and documented within 1 hour.

## **4.4 Restraint Policy**

Physical restraints are a last resort and may only be applied when a patient poses imminent risk of self-harm or harm to others, and after all alternatives have been exhausted. Restraint application requires:

**•** Physician order obtained before application (verbal order acceptable in emergency — written order within 1 hour).

**•** Informed consent obtained from patient or surrogate (document if consent not obtainable due to emergency).

**•** Type, reason, and time of application documented in EHR immediately.

**•** Patient assessed every 15 minutes: circulation, skin integrity, comfort, and need to continue.

**•** Physician reassessment and renewed order required every 24 hours.

**•** Restraints released, patient repositioned, and range-of-motion exercises performed every 2 hours.

# **5\. Pharmacy and Medication Management**

## **5.1 Medication Reconciliation**

Medication reconciliation is performed at three transition points: admission, transfer between units, and discharge. The pharmacist or pharmacy technician verifies the patient's home medication list against hospital orders. Discrepancies are reviewed and resolved by the prescribing physician within 4 hours.

## **5.2 Formulary Management**

**•** MGH maintains a hospital formulary reviewed annually by the Pharmacy and Therapeutics (P&T) Committee.

**•** Non-formulary medications require pre-authorisation from the P&T Committee or on-call pharmacist.

**•** Therapeutic substitution is permitted under standing protocol approved by the Medical Executive Committee.

**•** All new medications added to the formulary undergo a full safety and cost-effectiveness review.

## **5.4 Medication Error Reporting**

All medication errors — including near-misses — must be reported in the Safety Event Reporting System (SERS) within 1 hour of discovery. The report must include the drug, dose, route, time, patient identifiers (without PHI beyond MRN), the nature of the error, any intervention taken, and patient outcome. Root cause analysis is initiated for all errors reaching the patient (Category C–I).

# **6\. Surgical and Procedural Services**

## **6.1 Surgical Safety Checklist (WHO Standard)**

The WHO Surgical Safety Checklist is mandatory for all operative and invasive procedures. It is completed in three phases:

**•** Sign In: Before anaesthesia induction — patient identity, site, consent, equipment, allergies, anaesthesia safety check.

**•** Time Out: Before skin incision — team introduction, procedure confirmation, antibiotic timing, imaging displayed.

**•** Sign Out: Before patient leaves OR — instrument/swab/needle count confirmed, specimen labelled, equipment problems noted.

## **6.2 Surgical Site Infection (SSI) Prevention**

**•** Prophylactic antibiotics administered within 60 minutes of incision (120 minutes for vancomycin/fluoroquinolones).

**•** Antibiotics discontinued within 24 hours post-operatively (48 hours for cardiac procedures).

**•** Clipping (not shaving) used for hair removal immediately pre-op.

**•** Normothermia maintained intraoperatively; warming blankets applied.

**•** Glucose monitored post-op; maintained < 180 mg/dL in all diabetic and cardiac surgery patients.

## **6.3 Consent for Surgery**

Valid surgical consent requires: the patient to be an adult with decision-making capacity, the surgeon (not a delegate) to have conducted the consent discussion, all risks and alternatives to have been explained in plain language, and the patient to have had opportunity to ask questions. Consent must be documented in the EHR and signed forms scanned before the patient enters the OR.

# **7\. Patient Privacy, HIPAA and Data Security**

## **7.1 Protected Health Information (PHI)**

PHI encompasses any individually identifiable health information in any form — electronic (ePHI), paper, or verbal. This includes name, date of birth, address, diagnosis, treatment information, financial data, MRN, Social Security Number, and any other data that could identify a patient.

## **7.2 Minimum Necessary Standard**

**•** Access PHI only to the extent necessary for your assigned role and current patient care responsibility.

**•** Do not access records of patients not under your direct care — including family members of staff.

**•** EHR audit logs are reviewed monthly by the Compliance team; inappropriate access triggers disciplinary review.

**•** 'Breaking the glass' (overriding restricted access) is logged and reviewed within 24 hours.

## **7.4 Breach Notification Protocol**

**1\.** Suspected breach reported to Privacy Officer within 1 hour of discovery by any staff member.

**2\.** Privacy Officer initiates formal risk assessment within 24 hours using the four-factor HHS analysis.

**3\.** If breach confirmed: affected individuals notified in writing within 60 calendar days.

**4\.** Breaches affecting ≥ 500 residents of a state reported to HHS Secretary and prominent media simultaneously.

**5\.** All breaches logged in the Breach Register; root cause analysis and corrective action plan documented.

## **7.5 ePHI Security Controls**

**•** All workstations auto-lock after 5 minutes of inactivity; screensavers activated.

**•** Multi-factor authentication (MFA) mandatory for all EHR and remote access accounts.

**•** PHI must not be transmitted via unencrypted email; use the hospital's secure messaging platform.

**•** USB and external media transfers prohibited on clinical workstations.

**•** Annual HIPAA Security Risk Assessment conducted by the IT Security team and reviewed by Compliance.

## **8.2 Rapid Response Team (RRT)**

Any staff member — clinical or non-clinical — may activate the RRT by dialling the RRT extension (ext. 5555). No prior approval from a supervisor is required. RRT activation criteria include:

**•** Acute change in mental status or level of consciousness

**•** Heart rate &lt; 40 or &gt; 130 bpm

**•** Respiratory rate &lt; 8 or &gt; 28 breaths/min

**•** SpO₂ < 90% on supplemental oxygen

**•** Systolic BP < 90 mmHg unresponsive to fluid bolus

**•** Urine output < 50 mL over 4 hours in a catheterised patient

**•** Staff member is 'worried' about a patient — no objective threshold required

## **8.3 Disaster Preparedness**

MGH maintains a Hospital Emergency Operations Plan (HEOP) updated annually and tested via tabletop or full-scale exercises. All department heads are trained in the Hospital Incident Command System (HICS). Emergency supply inventory (water, medications, durable supplies) maintained for minimum 96-hour self-sufficiency.

# **9\. Infection Prevention and Control**

## **9.1 Hand Hygiene**

Hand hygiene is the single most effective measure to prevent healthcare-associated infections (HAIs). MGH adopts the WHO 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene framework, mandatory for all personnel who enter clinical areas.

**•** Before touching a patient

**•** Before a clean/aseptic procedure

**•** After body fluid exposure risk

**•** After touching a patient

**•** After touching patient surroundings

Compliance monitored via direct observation and ABHR consumption metrics. Target: ≥ 90% compliance. Departments below threshold receive mandatory retraining within 30 days.

## **9.3 Sharps and Biohazard Safety**

**•** Safety-engineered sharps devices used exclusively; recapping of needles is prohibited.

**•** Sharps containers disposed when three-quarters full; never overfilled or shaken down.

**•** Needlestick injuries reported to Occupational Health immediately; post-exposure prophylaxis offered within 2 hours.

**•** All biological waste segregated in red biohazard bags; disposed via contracted medical waste vendor.

# **10\. Laboratory and Diagnostic Services**

## **10.1 Specimen Collection Standards**

**•** Specimens labelled at bedside immediately after collection using patient-verified ID — never pre-labelled.

**•** Two patient identifiers confirmed verbally before specimen draw: full name and date of birth.

**•** Haemolysed, clotted, or incorrectly labelled specimens rejected and recollection ordered.

**•** Critical values telephoned directly to the ordering clinician within 15 minutes of result availability; read-back required.

# **11\. Patient Rights and Responsibilities**

## **11.1 Core Patient Rights**

In accordance with the Patient Bill of Rights (CMS Conditions of Participation §482.13), all patients at MGH are entitled to:

**•** Receive care in a safe setting, free from abuse, neglect, or exploitation

**•** Receive respectful care regardless of race, ethnicity, religion, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, or disability

**•** Be informed of their diagnosis, treatment plan, and prognosis in understandable terms

**•** Participate in decisions about their care, including the right to refuse treatment

**•** Have pain assessed and managed effectively

**•** Access an interpreter or communication aid at no cost if needed

**•** Privacy and confidentiality of medical records and personal information

**•** File a grievance without fear of retaliation; receive written response within 7 days

**•** Know the name and credentials of the healthcare providers treating them

## **11.2 Patient Responsibilities**

**•** Provide complete and accurate information about health history, medications, and allergies.

**•** Follow the agreed care plan; communicate concerns or changes in condition promptly.

**•** Treat staff and other patients with respect; harassment is not tolerated.

**•** Comply with visitor policies and no-smoking regulations on hospital premises.

**•** Meet financial obligations or notify the billing department to arrange a payment plan.

## **11.3 Grievance Process**

**1\.** Patient or family submits grievance verbally or in writing to the Patient Experience team.

**2\.** Acknowledgement letter sent within 3 business days.

**3\.** Investigation conducted; Patient Experience Coordinator interviews relevant staff within 5 days.

**4\.** Written resolution communicated within 7 calendar days of receipt of grievance.

**5\.** Unresolved grievances escalated to Chief Nursing Officer; external escalation to State Health Dept. as needed.

## **12.3 Sentinel Event Review**

Any sentinel event — defined as an unexpected occurrence resulting in death, serious physical injury, or psychological harm — triggers an immediate root cause analysis (RCA) conducted by the Patient Safety Committee. RCA findings and corrective action plans are presented to the Board Quality Committee within 45 days.

## **13.3 Performance Management**

**•** Annual performance reviews completed by direct supervisor using the MGH competency framework.

**•** Performance Improvement Plans (PIP) initiated for staff failing to meet two or more core competencies.

**•** Progressive discipline policy applies: verbal warning → written warning → final written warning → termination.

## **13.4 Staff Wellbeing**

MGH is committed to staff wellbeing and psychological safety. The Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) provides confidential counselling, financial advice, and crisis support free of charge to all employees and their immediate family members. Any staff member experiencing burnout, compassion fatigue, or mental health challenges is encouraged to contact the EAP (1-800-EAP-CARE) without fear of discrimination.

Harassment of any kind — sexual, racial, religious, or based on any protected characteristic — is strictly prohibited and subject to immediate investigation. Staff may report concerns anonymously via the Ethics Hotline (ext. 7777) or directly to the HR Director. Retaliation against anyone who reports harassment is a terminable offense.

## **14.2 Waste Management**

**•** Infectious waste: red biohazard bags; collected by licensed medical waste contractor.

**•** Sharps: puncture-resistant yellow sharps containers; removed when three-quarters full.

**•** General waste: black bags; standard municipal waste disposal.

**•** Pharmaceutical waste: segregated blue containers; returned to pharmacy for disposal.

**•** Radioactive waste (Nuclear Medicine): handled by licensed Radiation Safety Officer per NRC regulations.

## **14.3 Maintenance and Equipment Safety**

Preventive maintenance (PM) for all clinical equipment is scheduled and tracked by the Biomedical Engineering (Biomed) team. Equipment removed from service for repair must be tagged 'Do Not Use' and removed from the clinical area. Any equipment failure causing or potentially causing patient harm is reported in SERS within 1 hour.

# **15\. IT, Cybersecurity and EHR Governance**

## **15.1 Access Control**

**•** Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) applied to all hospital information systems; access provisioned by IT upon HR notification of hire.

**•** Shared or generic accounts are prohibited; each user must have unique login credentials.

**•** Access suspended immediately upon staff departure; accounts deleted within 48 hours of separation.

**•** Privileged admin accounts require additional MFA and are reviewed quarterly.

## **15.2 Cybersecurity Incident Response**

**1\.** Staff detecting unusual system behaviour, phishing attempts, or suspected ransomware immediately call the IT Security Hotline (ext. 4444).

**2\.** IT Security isolates affected systems within 15 minutes of confirmed incident.

**3\.** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) and Privacy Officer notified within 30 minutes.

**4\.** Business continuity downtime procedures activated if EHR unavailable > 30 minutes.

**5\.** Incident report filed with HHS if ePHI potentially compromised; FBI notified for ransomware per federal guidance.

## **15.3 Acceptable Use Policy**

**•** Hospital IT assets (computers, network, email) are for business use only; personal use is prohibited.

**•** Installation of unauthorised software is prohibited; all software requests submitted via IT Help Desk.

**•** Browsing to social media, gaming, or streaming sites on hospital networks is blocked and monitored.

**•** All outbound emails containing PHI must use the hospital's encrypted email gateway.

**•** Annual cybersecurity awareness training mandatory for all staff; phishing simulation conducted quarterly.

# **16\. Data Governance for AI / RAG Chatbot System**

## **16.1 Purpose of the RAG Knowledge Base**

The hospital's AI-powered Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) chatbot system provides on-demand access to policy and procedural information for authorised staff. The chatbot retrieves content from this document and other approved knowledge base sources to answer queries accurately and with source attribution. The system does not generate clinical diagnoses, prescriptions, or personalised medical advice.

## **16.2 Document Classification for RAG Ingestion**

| **Classification Tier** | **Examples**                                     | **RAG Access Level**      | **Permitted Chatbot Users**  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Public                  | Visitor FAQs, facility maps, general health info | Full retrieval            | All users including patients |
| ---                     | ---                                              | ---                       | ---                          |
| Internal                | SOPs, training materials, this policy document   | Full retrieval            | Authenticated staff only     |
| ---                     | ---                                              | ---                       | ---                          |
| Confidential            | HR records, vendor contracts, financial reports  | Restricted retrieval      | Role-based (manager+)        |
| ---                     | ---                                              | ---                       | ---                          |
| Restricted / PHI        | Patient records, identifiable health data        | Excluded — never ingested | Not accessible via chatbot   |
| ---                     | ---                                              | ---                       | ---                          |

## **16.3 Knowledge Base Update and Maintenance Protocol**

**1\.** Policy owners submit updated documents to the Knowledge Management (KM) team via the Document Management System (DMS) at least 30 days before effective date.

**2\.** KM team validates document for formatting, completeness, and metadata tags (category, owner, effective date, review date, policy number).

**3\.** Policy Review Committee approves content within 10 business days.

**4\.** Approved documents ingested into vector database (Pinecone / Weaviate or equivalent); embedding freshness verified within 24 hours.

**5\.** Superseded versions flagged 'retired' in the DMS; removed from the active RAG retrieval index immediately upon new version go-live.

**6\.** Emergency policy updates (e.g., outbreak guidance) fast-tracked within 4 hours with written approval from CMO or designee.

## **16.4 RAG System Quality and Safety Controls**

**•** All chatbot responses cite the source document title, section, and policy number.

**•** Clinical information responses carry a mandatory disclaimer: 'This information is for reference only. Always apply clinical judgment and consult your supervisor for patient-specific decisions.'

**•** Hallucination monitoring: weekly sampling of 50 chatbot query-response pairs reviewed by the Clinical Informatics team.

**•** Out-of-scope queries (clinical diagnoses, drug dosing for named patients, legal advice) automatically declined with escalation message.

**•** Chatbot interaction logs retained for 7 years per the Document Retention Policy (MGH-POL-2023-009); logs do not contain PHI.

**•** Annual AI governance review by the Clinical AI Ethics Board; findings reported to the Board of Trustees.

## **16.5 Chatbot User Responsibilities**

Authorised users of the RAG chatbot system must:

**•** Use the chatbot only for work-related queries within their scope of practice.

**•** Not enter patient names, MRNs, or any PHI into chatbot query fields.

**•** Report inaccurate or outdated responses to the Knowledge Management team via the in-app feedback button.

**•** Not share chatbot-generated outputs externally without review by the appropriate department head.
