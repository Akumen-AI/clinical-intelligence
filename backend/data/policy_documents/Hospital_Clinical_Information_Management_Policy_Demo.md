## 2. Purpose

This policy establishes a consistent framework for managing patient clinical information throughout its lifecycle. It is designed to protect patient safety, confidentiality, integrity, availability, and lawful use while enabling clinicians and authorized staff to access accurate information when needed for care.

The policy also establishes controls for paper records, scanned documents, electronic health records, diagnostic reports, prescriptions, clinical notes, and information processed by approved digital or artificial intelligence systems.

---

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All patient-identifiable and patient-linked clinical information created, received, stored, processed, transmitted, or disclosed by the hospital.
- Paper records, scanned images, PDFs, photographs of clinical documents, electronic records, structured data, messages, and system-generated reports.
- All hospital departments and care settings, including outpatient, inpatient, emergency, diagnostic, pharmacy, nursing, administration, and medical records functions.
- Third-party systems and service providers that process hospital information under an approved agreement.

This policy does not replace applicable law, professional standards, clinical protocols, emergency procedures, or specific information-security standards. Where requirements conflict, the stricter applicable requirement should be followed and escalated to the appropriate authority.

---

## 4. Core Principles

The hospital will manage clinical information according to the following principles:

- **Patient safety first.** Information controls must support timely and safe clinical decision-making.
- **Minimum necessary access.** Staff receive access appropriate to their role and legitimate work requirement.
- **Accuracy and traceability.** Clinical information must be attributable, time-stamped where applicable, and protected against unauthorized alteration.
- **Confidentiality.** Patient information must only be accessed, used, or disclosed for authorized purposes.
- **Availability.** Authorized users must be able to obtain necessary information when required for care, subject to approved downtime procedures.
- **Human accountability.** Automation and AI may assist information processing, but accountable staff remain responsible for clinical decisions and record verification.
- **Privacy by design.** New workflows and technology should minimize unnecessary collection, exposure, duplication, and retention of patient information.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

### 5.1 Medical Superintendent / Governance Committee

- Approves this policy and major changes to clinical information governance.
- Ensures organizational accountability for patient information management.
- Reviews significant privacy, security, data-quality, and patient-safety incidents.

### 5.2 Health Information Management Department

- Maintains the official record-management framework and retention schedule.
- Oversees record completeness, indexing, correction workflows, release of information, and archival processes.
- Coordinates record-quality audits and supports investigations.

### 5.3 Clinical Staff

- Create complete, timely, accurate, and clinically relevant documentation.
- Verify information before relying on it for clinical decisions.
- Report suspected errors, missing records, privacy incidents, and unsafe information.

### 5.4 IT / Information Security

- Implements access control, authentication, audit logging, backup, recovery, monitoring, and security controls.
- Manages system availability and approved downtime procedures.

### 5.5 All Users

- Use only their own credentials and protect authentication information.
- Access patient information only for legitimate work purposes.
- Immediately report suspected unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, alteration, or system compromise.

## 6. Clinical Information Lifecycle

### 6.1 Creation and Intake

- Information should be created at the point of care or as soon as reasonably practicable afterward.
- Documents received from external sources must be associated with the correct patient using approved identifiers and verification controls.
- Scanned or photographed documents must be legible, complete, correctly oriented, and linked to the correct patient and encounter.
- Duplicate, misfiled, or incorrectly attributed records must be quarantined from clinical use and corrected through the approved workflow.

### 6.2 Verification and Data Quality

- Patient identity must be verified using at least the hospital-approved identifiers before a document is filed or acted upon.
- Clinically significant extracted or transcribed information must be reviewed by an authorized human before it becomes part of the authoritative clinical record.
- Corrections must preserve the original information or an auditable history of the change, as required by the record system.

### 6.3 Storage and Classification

- Authoritative clinical records must be stored in hospital-approved systems or approved physical archives.
- Patient information must not be stored in personal email accounts, consumer cloud storage, unmanaged devices, or personal messaging applications.
- Sensitive exports and removable media require explicit authorization and appropriate protection.

### 6.4 Access and Use

- Access is granted according to job role, clinical responsibility, and business need.
- Users must not browse records of themselves, family members, friends, colleagues, or public figures unless their role and current work assignment specifically require it.
- Shared accounts are prohibited except for formally approved technical or operational use cases with equivalent accountability controls.

### 6.5 Disclosure and Sharing

- External disclosure requires a valid legal, clinical, operational, or patient-authorized basis, as applicable.
- Only approved channels may be used to transmit patient information.
- Requests for records must be authenticated and documented according to the hospital release-of-information procedure.

### 6.6 Retention and Disposal

- Records are retained according to the hospital retention schedule and applicable legal requirements.
- Records must not be destroyed merely because they are inconvenient, duplicated, or difficult to retrieve.
- Disposal must prevent reconstruction or unauthorized recovery, including secure shredding for paper and approved secure deletion or destruction for electronic media.

## 7. Use of AI, OCR, and Automated Information Processing

The hospital may use approved artificial intelligence, optical character recognition, summarization, retrieval, classification, or decision-support capabilities to improve administrative and clinical information workflows.

Such systems must be governed as information-processing systems, not treated as inherently authoritative sources.

### 7.1 Approved Systems Only

Patient information may only be processed by tools that have been reviewed and approved for the intended use.

### 7.2 Human Verification

OCR output, extracted entities, summaries, classifications, and generated reports must be verified before being treated as authoritative where an error could affect patient care, safety, billing, or legal records.

### 7.3 Source Traceability

AI-assisted outputs should retain a link or reference to the source record wherever technically feasible.

### 7.4 No Autonomous Clinical Decision-Making

AI output must not independently diagnose, prescribe, change treatment, or override a qualified clinician.

### 7.5 Prompt and Data Minimization

Users should provide only the information necessary for the approved task.

### 7.6 Auditability

Material AI-assisted actions should be logged sufficiently to reconstruct what system, source information, user, and workflow were involved.

> **Important:** AI-generated content that contains a clinically meaningful error must be corrected promptly, and the incident should be reported when it creates or could create patient-safety risk.

## 8. Information Security Controls

The hospital will maintain appropriate administrative, technical, and physical safeguards for clinical information.

- Unique user accounts and strong authentication are required for systems containing patient information.
- Access privileges must be reviewed periodically and removed promptly when staff change roles or leave the organization.
- Systems must maintain audit logs for access and material record changes where supported.
- Data should be encrypted in transit and at rest where supported by the system and risk assessment.
- Backups must be protected from unauthorized access and periodically tested for restoration.
- Security patches and supported software versions should be maintained according to the hospital's risk-based IT procedures.
- Printed records must not be left unattended in public or uncontrolled areas.
- Screens displaying patient information must be positioned or protected to reduce unauthorized viewing.

---

## 9. Incident Management

Any suspected privacy, security, integrity, availability, or patient-safety incident involving clinical information must be reported through the hospital's approved incident channel without undue delay.

Examples include:

- Wrong-patient filing, duplicate identity, or incorrect record association.
- Unauthorized access or disclosure.
- Lost or stolen device or paper record containing patient information.
- Accidental transmission to the wrong recipient.
- Unauthorized modification or deletion of a clinical record.
- AI/OCR extraction error that could materially affect patient care.
- Malware, ransomware, suspected credential compromise, or system outage affecting clinical information.

The response should prioritize:

1. Patient safety.
2. Containment.
3. Preservation of evidence.
4. Restoration of reliable information.
5. Appropriate notification.
6. Root-cause analysis.
7. Corrective action.

---

## 10. Downtime and Business Continuity

- When an electronic clinical information system is unavailable, staff must use the approved downtime procedure.
- Critical clinical information should be recorded using approved downtime forms or systems.
- Once systems are restored, downtime information must be reconciled into the authoritative record with appropriate verification.
- Staff must not create unofficial shadow databases as a substitute for the hospital record system.

---

## 11. Training, Monitoring, and Audit

- Personnel with access to patient information must complete required privacy, information-security, and documentation training.
- Training must be refreshed periodically and when material policy or system changes occur.
- The hospital may audit access logs, record quality, release-of-information activity, and system use to identify inappropriate access or process failures.
- Repeated or serious violations may result in corrective action under applicable employment, professional, contractual, and legal procedures.

---

## 12. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must be documented, risk-assessed, approved by the designated authority, and time-limited where feasible.

Emergency access may be granted when necessary to protect patient safety, but the reason for access must be documented and subject to subsequent review.

---

## 13. Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed at least annually and sooner when there are significant changes in:

- Law or regulation.
- Clinical practice.
- Information systems.
- Cybersecurity risk.
- Organizational structure.
- Hospital use of AI and automated processing.

# Appendix A. Minimum Clinical Record Quality Checklist

Before a document or extracted record is treated as part of the authoritative clinical record, the responsible user should confirm:

- Correct patient identity and encounter.
- Document type and date are correctly captured.
- Pages are complete and legible.
- Names, medications, doses, dates, values, and other clinically significant fields are accurately transcribed or extracted.
- Any AI/OCR-generated information has been reviewed where required.
- Source document remains available or traceable according to the workflow.
- Corrections are made through the approved correction process.
