# Standard Operating Procedure: Patient Handoff and Shift Change

## Section 1: Purpose

This SOP defines the mandatory process for transferring patient care responsibility between nursing staff at shift change, and between departments during patient transfer, to ensure no loss of critical clinical information.

## Section 2: Scope

This procedure applies to all registered nurses, licensed practical nurses, and charge nurses across all inpatient departments. It does not apply to outpatient or same-day discharge encounters, which are covered under SOP-NUR-021.

## Section 3: Handoff Timing

3.1. Shift handoff must begin no earlier than 15 minutes before the scheduled shift end and must be completed before the outgoing nurse leaves the unit.

3.2. Handoff for a patient being physically transferred between departments (e.g. Emergency to ICU) must occur at the bedside, not by phone or written note alone.

3.3. If the outgoing nurse is unavailable due to an emergency, the charge nurse must complete the handoff using the patient's most recent documentation and flag the incomplete handoff in the shift log.

## Section 4: Required Handoff Content

Every handoff must verbally and in writing cover the following, using the SBAR format (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation):

4.1. Current diagnosis and reason for admission.

4.2. Active medications administered in the current shift, including any PRN (as-needed) doses given and remaining allowable doses.

4.3. Any pending lab or imaging results not yet reviewed by the physician.

4.4. Allergies and any adverse reactions observed during the shift.

4.5. Fall-risk status and any mobility restrictions.

4.6. Family communication status — whether next of kin has been updated on condition changes.

## Section 5: Documentation Requirements

5.1. The outgoing nurse must complete the electronic shift note before handoff begins.

5.2. The incoming nurse must countersign the handoff record within 30 minutes of shift start.

5.3. Any discrepancy identified between verbal handoff and written documentation must be resolved before the outgoing nurse leaves the premises, and logged as a handoff variance.

## Section 6: Escalation

6.1. If a handoff cannot be completed due to missing information (e.g. lab results not yet available), the incoming nurse must escalate to the charge nurse before assuming full responsibility for the patient.

6.2. Repeated handoff variances (3 or more in a 30-day period) for the same nurse must be reported to the Director of Nursing for a process review.
